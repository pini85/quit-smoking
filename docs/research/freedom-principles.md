# Freedom Principles — Research Corpus

This document is the content source-of-truth for the "Freedom" feature (the Allen
Carr-inspired belief system in Unsmoke). Later tasks (content, copy, tone review,
content tests) cite this document by section anchor — do not renumber or rename
the headings below without updating those citations.

It transcribes the research corpus assembled for Task 1: 17 Carr principles, an
18-row belief catalog, 8 behavioral-science mechanisms, the evidence status of the
Easyway method itself, and the tone doctrine that governs all "Freedom" copy. It
also formalizes the classification system used to keep persuasive framing and
medical fact clearly separated, and closes with the decisions and postponements
made while scoping Task 1.

## Classification key

Every claim of substance in this document (and in any copy derived from it) is
tagged with one or more of these three labels:

- **[CARR]** — Carr conceptual framing. A persuasive mental model from Allen
  Carr's Easyway method. Not clinically validated on its own terms, even where it
  converges with real psychology. Useful, honest-if-labeled, but not a fact.
- **[PSYCH]** — Peer-reviewed behavioral psychology. An empirical finding or
  mechanism from the behavioral-science literature (conditioning, cognitive
  psychology, addiction research).
- **[MED]** — Medical consensus. An established clinical/physiological fact
  (timelines, health effects, treatment efficacy) commonly agreed upon in
  medicine.

**Binding rule: the app must never present [CARR] as medical fact.** Where a
principle blends registers (e.g. a true physiological timeline wrapped in a Carr
metaphor), this document tags the blend explicitly (e.g. "Hybrid" or "[CARR]+[MED]")
so downstream copy can keep the persuasive frame and the clinical fact
distinguishable to a reviewer, even when both appear in the same sentence to the
user.

## A. Carr principles

Seventeen core reframes from Allen Carr's Easyway method. For each: what it
means, the belief it challenges, where it shows up in the app, the app
translation, its classification, and its source.

### A1. Little Monster

**What it means:** Physical nicotine withdrawal is a faint physical restlessness,
not pain. It "dies" within days, and smokers already carry it all day, every day,
without noticing — it's the backdrop of a smoker's life, not a foreground event
unique to quitting.

**Challenges:** The belief that "withdrawal will be unbearable."

**Classification:** Hybrid. The physiological timeline — withdrawal peaks around
day 3 and resolves over 2–4 weeks — is **[MED]**. The "no pain" framing and the
personification of withdrawal as a small, dying creature is **[CARR]**.

**App translation:** Copy can state the timeline as fact but must render the
lived experience as mild and finite, never as suffering to be endured.

### A2. Big Monster

**What it means:** The real trap isn't the physical withdrawal signal itself —
it's the belief system built on top of it: years of conditioning and cultural
messaging that interpret the Little Monster's faint signal as "I need a
cigarette." The Big Monster is psychological, and it's the one worth dismantling.

**Challenges:** The belief that "my addiction is chemical" (and therefore outside
the smoker's control to reframe).

**Classification:** **[CARR]**, though it maps onto a real **[PSYCH]** construct —
outcome expectancies, as measured by instruments like the Brandon & Baker Smoking
Consequences Questionnaire (SCQ). Carr's claim that quitting is "99% mental" is
his own framing, not a medical finding.

**App translation:** Used for the `confidence` and `always-want` beliefs — the
idea that some smokers are more "addicted" than others, or will always
occasionally want one, is a belief-system effect, not a fixed chemical fact.

### A3. Illusion of pleasure

**What it means:** A cigarette doesn't create pleasure — it relieves the
discomfort that the previous cigarette created, returning the smoker only to the
baseline a non-smoker already enjoys all the time. The relief is misread as
enjoyment (Carr's "tight shoes" analogy: taking off shoes that are too tight
feels good, but it isn't a positive sensation created by the shoes). The tell:
the "best" cigarettes are the ones after the longest gaps — because that's when
the manufactured discomfort is greatest.

**Challenges:** The belief that smoking is genuinely pleasurable, particularly
tied to `relaxation`, `coffee-ritual`, `meal-completion`, `reward`,
`deprivation`, and `life-worse`.

**Classification:** **[CARR]** framing, supported by the deprivation-reversal
model (Parrott 1994/2012 — **[PSYCH]/[MED]**). The universal claim that there is
"no pleasure at all, ever" is Carr's lens on the data, not the data itself.

**App translation:** Reframe "I enjoyed that cigarette" moments as relief from a
manufactured dip, not evidence of real pleasure — and note the longest-gap tell
as a diagnostic the user can notice for themselves.

### A4. Stress-relief illusion

**What it means:** Nicotine withdrawal itself operates as a low-grade background
stressor between cigarettes. Each cigarette "relieves" the stress that the
addiction itself manufactured — smoking isn't calming the smoker down from a
neutral baseline, it's removing a tension it created.

**Challenges:** `relaxation` and `stress-relief` beliefs.

**Classification:** Unusually strong support for a Carr-originated claim: Taylor
et al., *BMJ* 2014, and the Cochrane 2021 review both find that quitting smoking
*reduces* anxiety, depression, and stress, with an effect size comparable to
antidepressants — **[MED]**. The app can be assertive here; this is one of the
few places Carr's intuition and the clinical literature align closely.

**App translation:** State directly and confidently that quitting reduces
stress and anxiety over time — this claim carries real medical backing, not
just persuasive framing.

### A5. Concentration illusion

**What it means:** Falling nicotine levels between cigarettes create a mild
attentional dip; the next cigarette then "fixes" the very problem it caused,
which is misread as smoking aiding concentration.

**Challenges:** The `concentration` belief ("smoking helps me focus").

**Classification:** **[CARR]** + **[MED]**. Concentration genuinely dips briefly
during withdrawal (a real, medically recognized effect) and then returns to
baseline or better; the app must not claim nicotine has zero acute attentional
effect. The honest line is that smoking "fixes a problem it created," not that
the effect on concentration was ever illusory in the sense of not existing.

**App translation:** Acknowledge the real short-term withdrawal dip in focus
honestly, while reframing what the cigarette actually restores.

### A6. Boredom illusion

**What it means:** Smoking is not itself interesting or engaging. Boredom is a
high-risk moment simply because it removes all the normal distractions that
would otherwise mask the faint Little Monster signal.

**Challenges:** `boredom-relief` and `break-permission` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]** framing of the illusion itself. Boredom as a
high-risk relapse context is independently supported by ecological momentary
assessment (EMA) studies — **[PSYCH]**.

**App translation:** Frame boredom moments as "nothing to mask the signal," not
as evidence smoking provides stimulation.

### A7. Nothing to give up

**What it means:** The signature Carr reframe. Quitting should be understood as
pure escape and pure gain — not sacrifice. Feeling deprived after quitting is
itself evidence of still believing (falsely) that the benefit smoking provided
was real. The word "giving up" presupposes a real loss that doesn't exist.

**Challenges:** `reward`, `deprivation`, and `life-worse` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]**; resonates with gain-framing research in the
persuasion literature — **[PSYCH]**.

**App translation:** Banish "giving up" from all copy, everywhere in the app, not
just in Freedom-feature content. Frame quitting exclusively as acquisition (time,
money, breath, control) rather than subtraction.

### A8. Willpower vs. removing desire

**What it means:** A willpower-based quit means wanting a cigarette forever and
fighting that want indefinitely — an exhausting, unstable state. Easyway's
alternative is to remove the desire itself, so there's nothing left to resist or
white-knuckle through.

**Challenges:** The `willpower-needed` belief.

**Classification:** **[CARR]**; related **[PSYCH]** finding: expectancy change
(changing what someone believes smoking will do for them) predicts cessation
better than trait willpower measures do.

**App translation:** Never congratulate the user for "resisting" or "being
strong" — celebrate *seeing through* the belief instead. This is a hard
constraint on all achievement/celebration copy, not just Freedom content.

### A9. Fear keeps smokers smoking

**What it means:** What keeps people smoking is fear — fear of life without the
crutch, fear of the quitting process itself, fear of failing again — and that
fear is manufactured by the addiction, not a rational response to quitting.

**Challenges:** `stress-relief` and `social-ease` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]**; converges with the Extended Parallel Process
Model (EPPM) literature on fear appeals — **[PSYCH]**.

**App translation:** No scare tactics anywhere in the app. Fear-based messaging
is understood (by this same research) to drive smoking behavior, not deter it.

### A10. "Just one" trap

**What it means:** The real question is never "should I have just one
cigarette" — it's "am I choosing to be a smoker again, all day, every day, for
life?" A single puff after quitting is a strong predictor of full relapse.

**Challenges:** The `just-one` belief.

**Classification:** **[CARR]** framing of the question; the predictive power of
a single lapse is supported by the medical/psychological lapse literature
(**[MED]/[PSYCH]**). This principle must be paired with the abstinence violation
effect (see **C7**) so that an actual lapse is never framed as inevitable doom —
prevention framing and post-slip framing are different jobs.

**App translation:** Use this framing prospectively, to help someone decline the
first cigarette. Never invoke it retrospectively to catastrophize an actual slip
— that job belongs to the app's existing slip-handling design, which this
content must not undermine.

### A11. Envy vs. pity

**What it means:** Relapse is often triggered by envying a smoker at a party or
social event. The reframe: invert the emotion. The still-smoking person envies
the ex-smoker, whether they'd admit it or not — every smoker, if honest, wishes
they'd never started. The refusal script matters too: "I don't smoke," not
"I'm trying to quit."

**Challenges:** `alcohol-pairing` and `social-ease` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]**. The refusal-script mechanism — "I don't" reads
as identity and is more resistant to erosion than "I can't," which reads as a
constraint under negotiation — is supported by Patrick & Hagtvedt 2012
(**[PSYCH]**).

**App translation:** Any scripted refusal language in the app should use "I
don't smoke" phrasing, never "I'm trying to quit" or "I can't."

### A12. Ritual pairings

**What it means:** Cigarettes attach themselves to genuinely good, unrelated
moments — coffee, alcohol, meals, work breaks, driving — and steal the credit
for feelings the moment itself already provided. The instruction is not to avoid
these moments, but to keep the ritual exactly as it is and notice that it's
already complete without the cigarette.

**Challenges:** `coffee-ritual`, `alcohol-pairing`, `meal-completion`, and
`break-permission` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]** framing; functionally this is cue-exposure /
extinction learning — **[PSYCH]** (see **C1**, **C3**).

**App translation:** Never suggest avoiding or changing the ritual context
(skip coffee, skip the bar). Instead, prompt the user to keep the ritual intact
and notice, in the moment, that it's already whole.

### A13. Immediate non-smoker identity

**What it means:** You become a non-smoker the instant the last cigarette is
finished — not after a probationary period of weeks or months. Keeping an
"emergency pack just in case" is itself an act of doubt that undermines the
identity shift.

**Challenges:** The `identity` belief.

**Classification:** **[CARR]**; aligns with identity-change research, notably
West's PRIME theory of motivation — **[PSYCH]**.

**App translation:** Careful — this principle must not conflict with the app's
existing honest post-slip streak accounting. Identity language ("you're a
non-smoker now") should coexist with truthful streak/lapse data, not paper over
it. See **Decisions & Postponements** below regarding SlipScreen belief tagging.

### A14. No cutting down, no substitutes

**What it means:** Tapering down gradually, rather than stopping outright, makes
each remaining cigarette scarcer and therefore more psychologically precious —
plausible via scarcity-effect research. Carr's method also opposes nicotine
substitutes (gum, patches, vapes) on the theory that they perpetuate the
addiction itself rather than resolving it.

**Challenges:** Underlies the broader `willpower-needed` and `deprivation`
belief patterns as a method-level claim rather than a single belief-catalog
entry.

**Classification:** The "tapering increases preciousness" claim is **[CARR]**,
plausible via scarcity effects. **The anti-NRT stance directly contradicts
medical consensus: NRT and varenicline are proven effective cessation aids per
Cochrane review evidence — [MED].**

**App translation: this is never presented as advice in the app.** The app must
never recommend against NRT, patches, gum, or varenicline, and must never
frame nicotine replacement as "still being addicted." A content/tone-guard test
enforces a banned-vocabulary list against this exact failure mode. See
**Decisions & Postponements**.

### A15. Don't suppress smoking thoughts

**What it means:** Actively trying not to think about smoking backfires —
suppressed thoughts tend to return with more force (the classic "don't think of
a white bear" effect). The alternative is to let the thought arrive and reframe
it on arrival: "isn't it marvellous — I don't need to do that anymore."

**Challenges:** `miss-it-forever` and `always-want` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]** + **[PSYCH]** — thought suppression backfiring is
a well-established finding (Wegner's ironic process theory).

**App translation:** Never build a feature that rewards "not thinking about
smoking" (e.g., streaks or scores for suppressing thoughts). Thoughts about
smoking are expected and fine; what matters is how they're reframed when they
arrive, not whether they occur.

### A16. Nostalgia / moping trap

**What it means:** Memory doesn't store a full, accurate record of what smoking
was actually like — it keeps a rose-tinted highlight reel of the good moments
and quietly drops the coughing, the smell, the cost, the health worry. The
antidote is deliberately recalling the whole chain of the habit, not just the
edited highlights.

**Challenges:** `identity` and `miss-it-forever` beliefs.

**Classification:** **[CARR]** framing; the underlying memory bias — rosy
retrospection / fading-affect bias — is independently documented **[PSYCH]**.

**App translation:** When nostalgia surfaces, prompt for the fuller picture
(the whole chain of a smoking episode, not just its curated best moment) rather
than arguing the good memory itself is false.

### A17. The trap model

**What it means:** Smoking is structured like a trap that feels like a free
choice — Carr's own analogy is a pitcher plant, which lures its prey in easily
and makes leaving hard. Nobody sits down and rationally decides to become a
lifelong smoker; the trap closes gradually, starting from something that looked
harmless or even appealing.

**Challenges:** `confidence` and `willpower-needed` beliefs — the idea that
being caught in the habit reflects a personal moral or willpower failing.

**Classification:** **[CARR]**; pairs naturally with self-compassion research on
shame and behavior change — **[PSYCH]**.

**App translation:** Guilt is misplaced under this model, so copy should be
shame-free throughout — the user was never the author of a bad decision, they
were caught in a design.

## C. Behavioral science

Eight behavioral-science mechanisms that underpin why the Freedom approach is
plausible, independent of Carr's own framing.

**C1. Cue-conditioning** — **[PSYCH, robust]**. Repeated pairing turns everyday
contexts (coffee, driving, a phone call) into conditioned stimuli for smoking;
across roughly 1.5 million puff-level trials for a pack-a-day smoker over 20
years, dopamine signaling shifts from the drug itself onto the predictive cue.
(Bevins & Palmatier 2004.)

**C2. Habit loops** — **[PSYCH]**. The standard cue → routine → reward loop.
The practical task is identifying which reward is actually operating in a given
loop (the break itself, the social contact, the pause) and preserving that
reward while removing the cigarette from the loop.

**C3. Extinction learning** — **[PSYCH] mechanism; clinical cue-exposure
therapy shows only modest effect sizes**. Each smoke-free exposure to a
previously smoking-linked cue functions as an inhibitory-learning "rep" that
weakens the cue-smoking association. It is context-specific — a "renewal
effect" means an association can resurface in a genuinely novel context even
after extinguishing elsewhere, which is worth flagging honestly. This is the
mechanism behind both **A12** (ritual pairings) and any "you already proved
this" style copy in the app.

**C4. Urge surfing** — **[PSYCH, promising]**. Bowen & Marlatt 2009 found
roughly a 26% reduction in smoking from brief urge-surfing instruction; the
proposed mechanism is a changed relationship to the urge itself, not
suppression of it. This already harmonizes with the app's existing UrgeWave
runner feature.

**C5. Cognitive restructuring** — **[PSYCH, established, core CBT mechanism]**.
The standard belief → examine → replace loop. This is the honest scientific
description of why the Easyway method plausibly works at all — every belief in
the catalog (section **B**) becomes, functionally, a restructuring card.

**C6. Implementation intentions** — **[PSYCH, strong evidence base for
smoking cessation specifically]**. If-then contingency plans ("if X happens,
then I will do Y"). McWilliams 2019's meta-analysis found an overall odds ratio
of 1.70. Noted here as a candidate mechanism for a later release; not part of
this task's scope.

**C7. Abstinence violation effect (AVE)** — **[PSYCH]** (Marlatt & Gordon).
After a lapse, attributing it internally, stably, and globally ("I have no
willpower, I always fail, this is who I am") is what converts a single lapse
into a full relapse. **Design law: Carr's "there's no such thing as just one"
(A10) is prevention framing only — it is never to be applied after an actual
slip has occurred.** Post-slip, the app must use situational attribution
("that specific moment was hard") with zero catastrophizing. The app's existing
slip-handling behavior already implements this; Freedom content must not
contradict or undermine it.

**C8. Deprivation reversal / mental-health gains** — **[MED]**. Parrott
1994/2012; Taylor, *BMJ* 2014; Cochrane 2021. These are the safest "Carr was
right about this" facts available — quitting measurably reduces anxiety,
depression, and stress rather than increasing it.

## D. Easyway evidence status

The evidence status of the Easyway method itself, as distinct from the
individual psychological mechanisms it invokes (section **C**).

Two randomized controlled trials exist: **Keogan 2018** found roughly double
the quit rate of Quit.ie at 3 months; **Frings 2020** (*Addiction*, n=620) found
the method non-inferior to a gold-standard cessation service combined with
pharmacotherapy. A 2022 systematic review (*Tobacco Prevention & Cessation*)
covering six studies flagged bias concerns across the literature while
describing the seminar format as "promising." NICE recommends the in-person
Easyway seminar as an option in its evidence review (NG209). Critics
specifically fault the method's anti-NRT stance (see **A14**).

**Copy rule, binding on all Freedom content:** describe Easyway as "a
psychological approach with promising trial evidence, comparable in trials to
standard services." Never describe it as proven superior to standard
cessation methods, never present it as medical treatment, and never let it
carry anti-NRT advice into the app (see **A14** and **Decisions &
Postponements**).

## E. Tone doctrine

Binding on all Freedom-feature copy, not just this document's own prose.

- **Curiosity over combat.** Use notice, watch, see through, understand,
  unlearn. Never use battle, fight, resist, strength, or "beat" (the addiction,
  the urge, etc.).
- **No deprivation vocabulary.** Quitting, escaping, and "free from" are fine;
  giving up and sacrifice are not — banned outright.
- **No fear, no lectures.** Consistent with **A9**: fear drives smoking, so
  fear-based copy is self-defeating as well as against the doctrine.
- **Celebration from minute one.** Frame progress as gains acquired (time,
  money, breath, control), never as suffering being endured or survived.
- **Warmth for the trapped, contempt only for the trap.** Consistent with
  **A17**: the person was never the problem; the trap was.
- **Confident, plain, and slightly wry about reframes; scrupulously modest
  about medical claims.** The persuasive Carr material can be delivered with
  personality; anything tagged **[MED]** must be stated with appropriate
  clinical restraint and no more certainty than the underlying evidence
  supports.
- **Thoughts about smoking are fine.** The goal is not to eliminate thoughts
  about smoking (see **A15**) — it's that when they arrive, they land as "I'm
  free" rather than "I'm deprived."

## Decisions & Postponements

The following scope decisions were made in transcribing this corpus for Task 1.
Later tasks should treat each as settled unless explicitly revisited:

- **Anti-NRT framing is never presented as advice.** The app must never
  recommend against NRT, patches, gum, or varenicline, or frame their use as
  "still being addicted" (see **A14**). A content test enforces a banned
  vocabulary list against this failure mode.
- **Emotion taxonomy postponed.** No structured emotion classification is
  introduced by this task.
- **"Why do I want one?" introspection tool postponed.** Not built as part of
  this scope.
- **Notifications/push postponed.** No notification system exists in the repo
  today, so Freedom content does not assume or depend on one.
- **Daily booster reads are not persisted.** Any dismissal state is
  localStorage-only, not written to durable app state.
- **Post-craving belief tags write no automatic BeliefAssessment.** Tagging a
  belief during a craving flow does not itself create a persisted assessment
  record.
- **SlipScreen belief tagging postponed.** Not wired into the slip flow in
  this task (see the **A13** caution about not conflicting with honest
  post-slip streak accounting).
- **Freedom-related achievements postponed.** No new achievements are added
  for this feature in this task.
- **Belief "checkin" assessment context postponed.** Not introduced in this
  task.
- **New InsightRules for beliefs postponed.** Not introduced in this task.
